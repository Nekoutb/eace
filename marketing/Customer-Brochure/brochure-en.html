Why this matters to you

Paying a non‑compliant supplier can cost almost half the invoice.

In Cameroon, the General Tax Code makes the deductibility of your expenses and your VAT conditional on the supplier's tax compliance. If the supplier is untraceable, suspended or struck off — or if its Tax Compliance Certificate (ACF) is forged or expired — the administration can reject the expense and the VAT, then go further.

The real cost of a single rejection

33 %
Corporate tax on the reinstated, non‑deductible expense
+ 16.5 %
IRCM on the deemed hidden distribution
= 49.5 %
of the amount, before VAT, penalties & interest

A rejected expense can be reclassified as a hidden distribution of income and hit with IRCM. Together, the reassessment approaches half the invoice — on top of the rejected VAT (19.25%), penalties and late interest.

Example

On a 10,000,000 FCFA invoice paid to a struck‑off supplier: expense reinstated (≈ 3.3 M corporate tax), reclassification (≈ 1.65 M IRCM), VAT rejected (≈ 1.9 M) — nearly 6.9 M, before penalties. Avoidable in 30 seconds of verification.

What the platform does

4 checks, at 2 key moments — automatically.

In one sentence: the Elite Advisors Compliance Engine automatically verifies that every supplier you pay is in good standing with the tax authority, and keeps the proof. It returns a clear verdict — compliant or non‑compliant — with the supporting evidence.

The 4 checks

①

ACF authenticity

Is the Tax Compliance Certificate genuine? Verified live on the DGI's Harmony2 portal, with proof captured. Exposes forged certificates.

②

NIU status

Is the taxpayer active, suspended or struck off? The official status is read from the DGI's Fiscalis portal and captured as proof.

③

Presence in the register

Does the NIU appear in the DGI's official, up‑to‑date national taxpayer register?

④

ACF validity

Is the certificate still valid (issued + 3 months)? With a countdown and an alert before expiry.

And the invoice itself

Beyond the supplier, the platform reads the invoice and checks its form and substance against the Tax Code (art. 150): mandatory mentions, client identity, amounts. You know exactly why an invoice passes or fails.

No manual work

You upload the ACF and the invoice — the platform queries the official portals, runs the checks, captures the evidence and records the result. What used to take phone calls and PDFs becomes an instant, traceable control.

The 2 control phases

Protected at recording, and again at payment.

Compliance changes over time: a supplier in good standing at order time may no longer be so at payment, sometimes several weeks later. The platform protects you at both moments.

Phase 1 — at invoicing

Recording

When the invoice arrives, you scan the ACF and upload the invoice. The platform reads it, checks the supplier and captures the proof — before posting.

Phase 2 — at payment

Settlement

When paying (in batches if needed), the platform re‑checks. An expired ACF or a NIU that has become inactive blocks the payment — before the money leaves.

The benefit

You never pay on stale information. The check at settlement catches whatever has changed since recording.
